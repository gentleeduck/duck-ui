## Where does it run?

In your own process, against your own database, on Node 22 or newer or Bun; see [requirements](/duck-auth/installation#requirements). [Comparison](/duck-auth/comparison) sets it beside hosted services and other libraries.

## What should a production deploy do?

- Call `auth.strict({ env: 'production' })` at boot. It throws `AUTH_MISCONFIGURED` listing every setting that is unsafe in production.
- Apply the [compliance preset](/duck-auth/core/compliance) your scope needs.
- Read [Security](/duck-auth/advanced/security).

## Can I bring users from another system?

`auth.identities.bulkCreate` imports the people, matching existing ones by email; see [Identities](/duck-auth/core/identities#bulk-and-export). Password hashes come across as they are: a hasher that verifies both formats moves each one to yours at its owner's next sign-in; see [Passwords](/duck-auth/providers/password#bringing-hashes-from-another-system).

## Can several apps share users?

Engines built over the same stores share identities, credentials and sessions. To share one sign-in across subdomains, give the [cookie transport](/duck-auth/core/transports/cookie) a `domain`.

## Can I write my own provider or store?

Yes: see [providers](/duck-auth/providers#writing-your-own) and [adapters](/duck-auth/adapters#writing-your-own).

## Does it decide what a user may do?

No. duck-auth answers who the caller is; [duck-iam](/duck-iam/introduction) decides what they may do, and [Bridging to duck-iam](/duck-auth/guides/iam-bridge) wires the two together.

## Does it support machine-to-machine auth?

Yes: API keys, and an OAuth2 client-credentials exchange on top of them. See [API keys](/duck-auth/providers/api-key#client-credentials).

## How do I report a security issue?

Email `security@gentleduck.org`, not a public issue. The policy is in [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/gentleeduck/duck-iam/blob/main/packages/duck-auth/SECURITY.md).
